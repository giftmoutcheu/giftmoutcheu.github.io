|research⟩

IMT Atlantique · research paper · since October 2026

SWAP-based runtime assertions for debugging quantum programs

In progress

Measuring a quantum state changes it, so bugs can't be found by inspecting it. SWAP-based assertions check it mid-run, to detect and locate bugs.

Research question

Detection accuracy: how accurately does the assertion detect injected faults across different program locations?

Metric

ER = NincorrectNtotal > 5% → bug

Share of shots where the checked qubit does not read 0. Above 5%, the result is a real bug rather than normal hardware noise.

  1. Review

    Done

    State-of-the-art quantum assertions: statistical, dynamic and SWAP-based precise runtime assertions.

  2. Apply

    Under way

    SWAP-based assertions inserted into common quantum algorithms in Qiskit, building on hardware-friendly dynamic assertions.

  3. Evaluate

    Next

    Inject faults, then measure detection on the noisy IBM Aer simulator, and on real IBM hardware if time permits.

    Removed gatesWrong gates (X for Z)Reordered instructions

Perspectives · open directions beyond this paper, for future work

Assertions over groups of entangled qubitsA noise threshold computed per program, instead of a fixed 5%Cost of the extra gates on real hardwareAutomatic insertion of assertions from user specifications
